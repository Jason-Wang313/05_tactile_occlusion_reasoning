# Hostile Reviewer Response

The strongest hostile review says the comparison is unfair: dense entropy fails because the simulator gives it irrelevant decorative variables. The final response is to agree, expose that boundary, and then show why it matters. Dense contact-only removes decorative probes and reaches 1.000 success at budget four, the same as exact contact-equivalence. Full dense entropy remains at 0.083 because it spends its budget on decorative bits.

That result changes the claim. The paper should not claim a universal win over information gain or reconstruction. It should claim that dense hidden-state entropy is the wrong value-of-touch objective when visually hidden state contains variables that do not affect the pending manipulation contact.

The full-scale version adds the missing scope around that claim. It tests irrelevant-variable scaling, contact-class scaling, asymmetric noise, prior stress, probe-library ablations, expected-regret probing, sampled equivalence, and negative controls. The strongest positive result is still paired with its boundary: when irrelevant variables are absent or filtered, dense entropy succeeds too.

The remaining weaknesses are serious but now explicit: no hardware, no realistic 3D geometry, idealized tactile observations, and no learned visuo-tactile policy comparison. A fair review should evaluate the manuscript as a synthetic mechanism paper that isolates an objective mismatch, not as a deployed robotic tactile system.
